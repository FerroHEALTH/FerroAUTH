SMART tokens, whichever identity provider you run.

FerroSMART is the SMART App Launch 2.2 authorization server of the FerroHEALTH family, in pure Rust. Most identity providers cannot issue real SMART tokens: they drop the scopes and the launch context a health app asks for. People keep logging in where they do today, and FerroSMART issues the access tokens, with the patient in context and the scopes the person approved, for any FHIR or openEHR server.

Run the quickstart What FerroSMART is

One static binary on a distroless image beside PostgreSQL. Standards on every interface, and no claim or parameter of its own.

What it does

The authorization server SMART asks for

An app sends the person to FerroSMART. FerroSMART sends them on to your identity provider to log in, asks them which access to give, and hands the app a token the FHIR or openEHR server checks. The book cites the section of the specification behind each step.

SMART App Launch 2.2

Standalone and EHR launch

The authorization code flow with PKCE S256, the consent page, the patient picker of a standalone launch, and the patient and encounter context in the token response.

EHR launch API

An EHR hands over its context

The EHR registers the launch at POST /launch, or signs a launch JWT, and opens the app. The context reaches the app's token response, bound to the app and the server, once.

Backend Services

Systems without a person

The client credentials grant with an asymmetric client assertion (RS384 or ES384), scoped to what the client is registered for, for five minutes.

RFC 7662, RFC 7009

Refresh, introspection, revocation

Refresh tokens rotate on every use and a reused one revokes its grant. A resource server introspects a token; an app or a person revokes one.

BALP, EHDS

Its own audit trail

Every login and every token request is a FHIR AuditEvent committed with the token itself, read back by subject and time, and forwarded to your Audit Record Repository if you name one.

RFC 9068

Keys you rotate

Access tokens are signed JWTs. A next key is published before it signs and a previous key stays published after, so resource servers never meet a key they cannot find.

What it is not

Login stays where it is

FerroSMART keeps no password and runs no login page. It is an OpenID Connect client of your identity provider and reads only the claims you map: who the person is, their roles, and their fhirUser. It stores no clinical data; the patient picker reads the FHIR or openEHR server as the signed-in person.

In the family

The one FerroHEALTH product that issues tokens

The FerroHEALTH products reach each other only over published protocols. Each is its own SMART resource server through ferrohealth-smart, the crate this repository publishes, and trusts FerroSMART as an issuer under the ferrosmart preset.

FerroEHR

A token FerroSMART issues for an openEHR base carries the ehrId of the launch and SMART on openEHR scopes, which the CDR reads through ferrohealth-smart.

FerroBRIDGE

The FHIR R4 facade over openEHR describes FerroSMART in its own SMART document, and is the FHIR server of FerroSMART's conformance lane.

FerroTERM, FerroPIX and FerroFED

The terminology server, the patient index and the federation gateway trust FerroSMART the same way, with one issuer table, and keep their own rules on top of the scopes.

Protecting a server of your own in Rust? The resource-server checks are a crate on crates.io: 

cargo add ferrohealth-smart -F axum
